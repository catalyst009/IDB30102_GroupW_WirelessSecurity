# Expected Outcomes and Evaluation Metrics

### 1. Expected Research Outcomes
Based on the proposed methodology, the expected outcomes of this research include:
* **Wireless Threat Detection:** The proposed solution is expected to successfully distinguish between normal and suspicious or malicious wireless network activities (e.g., Rogue APs and Evil-Twins).
* **Improved Detection Performance:** The multi-sensor fusion approach is expected to demonstrate measurable performance improvements over the baseline using standard classification metrics.
* **Reduced False-Positive Rate:** By combining RSSI, CSI, and ToA, the proposed approach is expected to minimise incorrect alerts caused by environmental noise while maintaining effective detection.
* **Resource Efficiency:** The lightweight TinyML model is expected to operate within the limits of resource-constrained IoT endpoints, ensuring low processing time, CPU usage, and memory consumption.
* **Evaluation and Comparison:** A direct comparison between the single-feature baseline and the proposed multi-sensor approach will be produced.

### 2. Evaluation Metrics to be Used
The performance of the proposed TinyML classifier will be measured using the following binary classification and hardware efficiency metrics:
* **Accuracy:** The overall proportion of correctly classified legitimate vs. rogue frames.
* **Precision, Recall, & F1-score:** To evaluate the quality and reliability of the threat detection.
* **False Positive Rate (FPR):** Crucial for measuring the model's robustness against environmental noise (normal records incorrectly flagged as attacks).
* **Inference Latency:** The processing time required per frame on the ESP32 hardware.
* **Memory Usage:** The kilobyte (KB) footprint of the model to ensure it fits within microcontroller limits.
